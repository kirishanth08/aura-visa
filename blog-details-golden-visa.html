Greece vs. Portugal Golden Visa: Comparing 2026 Regulated Fund Routes

With direct residential real estate barred under Portugal's Mais Habitação law and Greece establishing tiered €800,000 metro limits, high-net-worth investors are pivoting to regulated private equity investment funds.

Dr. Fatima Al-Zahra
Head of Private Wealth & Investor Migration • Bar of Portugal & UAE

1. The Sunset of Direct Residential Real Estate in Portugal

Portugal's Golden Visa continues to rank as the world's most sought-after European residency-by-investment program, but the legal structure has fundamentally matured. The elimination of residential real estate has channelled 90%+ of foreign capital into CMVM-regulated venture capital and private equity funds.

To qualify under Article 3(1)(d), candidates must subscribe €500,000 into a fund with at least 60% capital invested in Portuguese-headquartered commercial enterprises. Crucially, fund units generate tax-exempt non-habitual distributions for non-residents and carry zero municipal property transfer taxes (IMT).

2. Greece's Tiered Real Estate Thresholds (€400k & €800k)

Tier 1 Prime Zones (€800,000)

Athens, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini, and islands with >3,100 population require minimum single-property transactions of €800,000.

Tier 2 Regional Zones (€400,000)

Secondary mainland municipalities and emerging rural regions retain accessible entry points of €400,000.

Portugal maintains a decisive advantage regarding physical presence: investors must spend only 7 days per year in Portugal to maintain residency and remain eligible for full European citizenship after 5 years. In Greece, permanent residency does not automatically lead to citizenship without residing full-time (7 continuous years).
